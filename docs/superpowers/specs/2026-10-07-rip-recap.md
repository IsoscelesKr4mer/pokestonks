## What the card actually contains

Read off the file, not recalled:

| block | content | derivable from the app today? |
|---|---|---|
| Header | product, rip date, packs, cards, autos | **no**, needs a rip |
| Box cost | `$299.99`, with a note about presale vs release | **no**, needs a rip |
| Book value | `$910.44`, "sum of live market on every card" | **no**, needs a value per card |
| Gross multiple | `3.03x`, and `2.57x after eBay fees` | derived from the two above |
| The 10 hits | player, parallel, value, colour dot | needs values; everything else exists |
| Best of the other 50 | next tier, seven chips | same |
| **The improbable part** | `Two Orange /25s. Orange is 1:384 packs. This box had 12. Pulling two is roughly a 1 in 550 box.` | **yes**, the odds sheet is already loaded |

**The part that got the praise is the part the app can already do.** "Two
Orange /25s, roughly a 1 in 550 box" is `parallels.oddsText` and a pack
count, nothing else. That is worth knowing before anything else gets built.

## Three things are missing, and only three

### 1. A rip

`cards.ripId` exists as a bigint column. There is no `rips` table, no
foreign key, and nothing writes it. It is a stub.

A rip is **not** a batch. A batch is a sitting of photographs, and after
the product-from-the-card work a sitting can hold cards from three
different sets. A rip is a thing you opened: one box, with a cost and a
pack count.

```sql
CREATE TABLE rips (
  id            bigserial PRIMARY KEY,
  user_id       uuid NOT NULL,
  product_id    bigint NOT NULL REFERENCES products(id),
  ripped_on     date NOT NULL,
  cost_cents    integer NOT NULL,
  pack_count    integer,
  cards_per_pack integer,
  source        text,          -- "presale", "Fred Meyer", "vending"
  notes         text,          -- "presale, plus tax. $329.99 on release day"
  created_at    timestamptz NOT NULL DEFAULT now()
);
```

`cost_cents` is integer cents, like everything else. `pack_count` is
nullable because a vending bundle is a rip too and its pack count is known,
while a repack is not.

**Where a rip gets created:** on the confirm screen, as one optional
question under the product hint. *"Was this a box you opened?"* opens three
fields (cost, packs, date, defaulting to the photos' EXIF date) and creates
the rip, attaching every card that commits from that batch. Not a separate
screen he has to remember: the moment he is already thinking about a box is
the moment he is confirming its photographs.

A card can also be attached to a rip afterwards, because he will backfill
2,800 photographs of past rips and those batches were never confirmed with
a cost.

### 2. A value per card

`estimatedValueCents` already exists in the scan contract
(`core/scan/result.ts`), is already validated as integer cents, and already
drives the `high-value` routing reason. **Nothing stores it.** It is parsed,
routed on, and dropped. `cards` has no value column at all.

So: `cards.estimated_value_cents integer` and
`cards.valued_at timestamptz`, both nullable, written at commit from the
scan's own reading.

**Where the number comes from, decided rather than asked:** the agent
session that is already looking at the photograph. There is no API key and
there never will be, and this is the same session that already reads the
serial, the parallel and the autograph off that same image. It is also what
Michael does by hand today, and the card's own footer is honest about what
it means: *"Values are live asking prices, not sales"*. The recap repeats
that line verbatim. A stale value is visible rather than silent because
`valued_at` is stored beside it.

### 3. The 1st Bowman count is currently a lie

The footer line is `17 of the 24 prospects are 1st Bowman. Arquette BCP-174
and Jesús Made BCP-244 are not.` That sentence is the single most
checkable claim on the card, and the app derives `isFirstBowman` from
`code.startsWith('BCP-')`, which would put all 24 at 1st Bowman and name
nobody as the exception.

See `2026-10-07-first-bowman-is-a-logo.md`. **That fix is a prerequisite for
this feature, not an unrelated cleanup.** Publishing a recap whose footer is
generated from a guess is how a false claim reaches an audience of 3,307.

## What the recap is, and is not

**It is a view, not an artifact.** `/rips/<id>` renders every block above
from live data, at any size. The 1600x900 export is one render of that view,
not a separate generator.

**It is not a collage builder.** The design spec also lists segmented
collages for Hits, Inserts, 1st Bowman, Base Rookies, Base Vets and
Prospects. Those are a second feature with their own needs (image
composition, layout per segment) and they are not what got the praise. They
come after.

## Build order

1. **Odds vs reality, against a rip he enters by hand.** One screen, no new
   value data, no 1st Bowman dependency. It produces "two Orange /25s in a
   12-pack box is a 1 in 550 box" from data already in the database. If this
   alone is good, the rest is worth building.
2. **The 1st Bowman fix**, because the footer depends on it.
3. **Value per card**, written at scan time.
4. **The full card**, every block, rendered at 1600x900 with a copy button.
5. **Collages**, if he still wants them.

## Open, and genuinely his call

**Does a rip get its cost from the pokestonks purchase ledger, or typed
fresh?** The box cost already exists as a `purchases` lot in pokestonks for
every box he has bought. Typing it again is duplication and a chance to get
it wrong; reading it means this app reaching into the other one. Both are
defensible and the answer changes what gets built, so it is not mine to
decide.

## Addendum: the headline block was prototyped against real data and works

Built as a throwaway (`scripts/tmp-rip-odds.mjs` in the Insights repo) and run
against product 49's live odds and the six parallels Michael pulled on
2026-09-10, read off his own rip card.

**"Odds vs reality" reproduces his numbers exactly.** Every line matches the
card verbatim:

```
  Blue Refractor      /150  1:64    expected 0.19 -> got 1
  X-Fractor           /-    1:6     expected 2.00 -> got 1
```

His card: `Blue /150 · 1:64 expected 0.19 -> got 1` and
`X-Fractor · 1:6 expected 2 -> got 1`. Nothing to design; the data is there.

**The headline block disagrees with him, and the app is right.**

| | expected in 12 packs | P(at least 2) | odds |
|---|---|---|---|
| his card | 0.0625 | 0.19% | 1 in 550 |
| computed | 0.127 | 0.74% | **1 in 136** |

He used the single `1:384` figure for "Orange" and asked the chance of
hitting it twice. But there are **four** Orange /25 parallels available in a
hobby pack (True Orange, Orange Wave, Reptilian Orange, Orange Shimmer), and
he pulled two different ones. "Two Orange /25s" is a claim about the tier, so
every way of hitting the tier counts, and the real event is about four times
likelier than he published.

This is the argument for building it rather than continuing by hand. The
claim went to 3,307 people. **Comp the tier, not the rung** applies to
probability exactly as it applies to price.

### Two implementation requirements this surfaced

**1. `odds_text` is multi-configuration and must be filtered by format.** The
column holds every print run's odds on one line:

```
"1:384 Hobby; 1:623 Breaker"
"1:156 Breaker"                 <- no Hobby figure at all
"1:3 Mega"
```

A recap for a Hobby box must read the Hobby clause and must treat a missing
clause as "not available in this configuration", not fall through to another
one. This is load bearing: Orange Geometric /25 is Breaker-only, and
including it would inflate the Orange tier by a fifth parallel that cannot
come out of a hobby pack.

**2. A tier is defined by print run, not by colour word.** Pool on
`print_run`, which is a number the database already holds, rather than on the
name. `Reptilian Orange` and `True Orange` are the same tier; `Orange
Geometric` is the same colour and a different configuration.

### Still missing for the rest of the card

Nothing above needs a value per card or a rips table. Both are still required
for book value, the multiple, the hits table and the value split, which is
why the build order in this spec puts the odds blocks first.
