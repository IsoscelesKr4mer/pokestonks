### The price ladder, and the double-count it caused

**Whatnot stamps the BUNDLE price onto every team that bundle produced.** The
rungs are **$280, $550, $810, $1,080**, climbing in steps of roughly $270.

I first read Kyle's six rows as six purchases and reported **$4,080**, calling
him the whale at 47.7% of the break. Michael explained the ladder. Three rows
at $810 and three at $550 are **two purchases, not six**, and the corrected
table inverts the conclusion:

| buyer | teams | paid | per team |
|---|---|---|---|
| bigcards1of1 | 2 | $1,890 | $945 |
| kylestr73803 | 6 | $1,360 | $227 |
| thecollectivecardvault | 2 | $700 | $350 |
| johnjordan777 | 1 | $550 | $550 |
| kevernja | 2 | $540 | $270 |
| jupiterjungle | 1 | $280 | $280 |
| tattooedtcg | 2 | $260 | $130 |

Break total **~$5,580**, not $8,560. **Kyle is the best value in the room**, not
the whale: $227 a team against a $349 average, because the ladder rewards going
deep. The actual spender is `bigcards1of1` at $945 a team.

**Rule: before summing a Whatnot buyer's rows, check whether the prices repeat.**
Repeating identical prices across several teams means one bundle, counted once.

### The other thing worth knowing

Kyle bought **breadth, not names**. He spent the most rows in the break and got
no marquee character: Vader, Luke and Yoda all went for **$260 to $280** to
other buyers while he paid $810 three times for faction bundles. That is a
real strategy in a wide set, but it is the opposite bet from buying icons.

### Caveat on the raw figures

The listing header read **Sold (32)** while only **16 distinct teams** appear,
one buyer and one price each. That gap is the spin-3-keep-2 mechanic: 32 spins
bought, 16 teams kept. So these are what the **kept teams cost**, not
necessarily what each buyer paid out. Anyone who spun and discarded is out more
than their row shows.
